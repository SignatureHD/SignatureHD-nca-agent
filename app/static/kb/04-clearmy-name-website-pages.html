An account on my report isn’t mine

How to find the exact entry, report possible fraud and lodge a free dispute.

1. Write down the details

The credit provider’s name, the date the account opened and the last 4 digits of the account number.

2. Check it really is not yours

Registered company names often differ from shop names. Old accounts you forgot about, or accounts opened in a joint name, can also show.

3. If you think it is fraud

Tell the credit provider in writing.

Open a case at your nearest police station and keep the case number.

Consider a protective listing with SAFPS so lenders take extra care with applications in your name.

4. Dispute it with the bureau

Get your free report from the bureau that shows the entry. You get one free report from each of TransUnion

, Experian

, XDS

and VeriCred

every 12 months.

Write down the credit provider, the last 4 digits of the account number and exactly what is wrong.

Lodge a free dispute with that bureau, online or by email. Attach your proof. Ask for a dispute reference number.

The bureau has 20 business days to check with the credit provider and either show evidence or correct the entry.

If nothing changes, or you disagree with the outcome, complain to the National Financial Ombud Scheme

. It is free.

Dear [Credit bureau]

I dispute the following entry on my credit report under section 72 of the National Credit Act:

Credit provider: [name on report]

Account number: [last 4 digits only]

I do not recognise this account and did not open it. Please investigate, ask the credit provider for evidence, and remove or correct the entry if it cannot be supported.

Please send me a dispute reference number.

Kind regards

[Your name]

Am I blacklisted?

There is no single blacklist in South Africa. Here is what lenders actually look at.

People say "blacklisted" when they are declined. In fact there is no blacklist. Lenders read your credit report and score, and decide based on their own rules.

What can count against you

Missed payments in your payment profile

Adverse classifications like "slow payer" or "handed over"

Judgments

A debt review flag

Too many recent enquiries

Not enough credit history at all

What to do

Get your free report from each bureau.

Find the entries above.

Dispute anything wrong. Wait out or pay what is accurate.

Watch out

Nobody can "remove you from the blacklist" for a fee. Accurate information stays for its legal period.

Clear your credit record

Find out what can be corrected, what will fall away with time, and what has to stay because it is true.

"Clearing your name" means different things for different entries. Some can be removed because they are wrong. Some fall away after a set time. Some must stay because they are accurate history. Knowing which is which saves you money and months of waiting.

Three kinds of entries

Wrong

An account that is not yours, a paid account showing a balance, a flag you never agreed to. These can be disputed for free.

Out of date

Information kept longer than the law allows, or not updated after you paid. These must be updated or removed.

Accurate

Late payments that really happened. These stay for the legal period. Nobody can lawfully remove them early.

What we do

Help you get your free credit report from each bureau.

Go through each entry and sort it into wrong, out of date or accurate.

Give you ready-to-send dispute messages for anything that is wrong.

How long things stay

The National Credit Regulations set maximum display periods. Enquiries show for up to 2 years. Your payment profile shows for up to 5 years. Adverse classifications such as "slow payer" show for up to 1 year. A judgment shows for up to 5 years, unless it is rescinded or paid first. Credit enquiry disputes

Check an enquiry you do not recognise before you challenge it.

Every time a lender checks your record for an application, an enquiry is added. Enquiries show for up to 2 years. Many enquiries in a short time can count against you. An enquiry you did not authorise can also be the first sign of identity fraud.

Check before you challenge

Company names on reports are often the registered name, not the shop name. "XYZ Finance" may be your furniture store.

Cellphone contracts, insurance quotes and some employers can also run checks.

If you still do not recognise it, treat it seriously.

If you did not authorise it

Contact the company and ask for proof of your consent.

Dispute the enquiry with the bureau.

If you think someone is using your identity, report it to the bureau and consider a protective listing with SAFPS (Southern African Fraud Prevention Service).

Dear [Credit bureau]

I dispute an enquiry on my credit report under section 72 of the National Credit Act:

Company that made the enquiry: [name on report]

Date of enquiry: [date]

I did not apply for credit with this company or give consent for this enquiry. Please ask them for proof of my consent and remove the enquiry if they cannot provide it.

Please send me a dispute reference number.

Kind regards

[Your name]

Credit repair vs debt review

Correcting information is not the same as dealing with too much debt.

Credit repair

Fixes wrong or outdated information on your report. Does not change what you owe.

Debt review

A legal process for over-indebted people. Lowers repayments through a court-ordered plan. You cannot take new credit while under review.

Which do you need?

If your report is wrong, start with disputes. If you cannot afford your repayments, talk to us about your options. Some people need both.

Debt review removal

Understand your status, the documents that matter, and the lawful way out that fits your situation.

The debt review flag comes off your record when the right document reaches the bureaus. Which document depends on where you are in the process. Be careful: debt review removal is one of the most common scams in credit repair.

Your situation

You have paid everything

Your debt counsellor must issue a clearance certificate (Form 19) and file it with the bureaus. The bureaus must then remove the flag.

Only your home loan is left

You can also qualify for a clearance certificate, as long as the rest is paid and your home loan is up to date.

You never agreed to it

Ask the debt counsellor for your signed application (Form 16). If there is none, complain to the NCR.

You want to leave early

This depends on whether a court order exists. Talk to us before you stop paying.

Ask for your clearance certificate

Dear [Debt counsellor]

I have paid all the debts in my debt review [or: all except my home loan]. Please issue my clearance certificate (Form 19) under section 71 of the National Credit Act and file it with the credit bureaus and the National Credit Regulator.

I attach the paid-up letters I have.

Kind regards

[Your name]

Red flags

Promises to remove debt review in days, upfront fees for "removal", or advice to stop paying your plan without a court process. These can leave you worse off.

Debt review status and clearance

What your status means, and what has to happen before the flag comes off.

The main steps

Application (Form 16).

You apply to a debt counsellor. Credit providers and bureaus are notified.

Assessment.

The counsellor decides whether you are over-indebted.

Restructuring.

A repayment plan is agreed and usually made a court or Tribunal order.

Payment.

You pay through a payment distribution agency.

Clearance (Form 19).

When you have paid, the counsellor issues a clearance certificate.

Clearance

Once you have paid all the debts in your plan, or all except your home loan, your debt counsellor must issue a clearance certificate and file it with the bureaus and the national credit register. The bureaus must then remove the record of your debt review.

Dear [Debt counsellor]

I have paid all the debts in my debt review [or: all except my home loan]. Please issue my clearance certificate (Form 19) under section 71 of the National Credit Act and file it with the credit bureaus and the National Credit Regulator.

I attach the paid-up letters I have.

Kind regards

[Your name]

If it is still showing

Ask your counsellor for proof they filed the certificate. Then send it to each bureau that still shows the flag as a dispute. If nothing happens, complain to the NCR.

Default removal

Check what kind of listing it is before you assume it can be removed.

"Default" is used loosely. On your report it may be an adverse classification such as "slow payer", "handed over" or "written off", or it may be a judgment, or simply missed payments in your payment profile. Each has different rules.

When a default can come off

It is wrong.

The account is not yours, the amount is wrong, or you were never in arrears. Dispute it.

You have paid it.

When you settle an account with an adverse listing, the credit provider must tell the bureaus within 7 days so the listing is removed. Get a paid-up letter and check your report.

It is past its display period.

Adverse classifications can only show for a limited time. If it is older, dispute it as outdated.

When it stays

If the listing is accurate and still within its display period, it stays. Anyone who promises to remove an accurate default for a fee is promising something the law does not allow.

What you can do

Get your free report from the bureau that shows the entry. You get one free report from each of TransUnion

, Experian

, XDS

and VeriCred

every 12 months.

Write down the credit provider, the last 4 digits of the account number and exactly what is wrong.

Lodge a free dispute with that bureau, online or by email. Attach your proof. Ask for a dispute reference number.

The bureau has 20 business days to check with the credit provider and either show evidence or correct the entry.

If nothing changes, or you disagree with the outcome, complain to the National Financial Ombud Scheme

. It is free.

Dear [Credit provider]

I settled account [last 4 digits only] on [date]. Please send me a paid-up letter confirming the account is settled with a zero balance, and update the information you send to the credit bureaus.

Kind regards

[Your name]

Fix your credit score

Separate the errors you can dispute from the habits that move your score, then work through them in order.

Your score is calculated from what is on your report. If the report is wrong, the score is wrong, so errors come first. After that, your score moves with how you use credit over time.

Step 1: fix errors

Check every account, balance and status. Dispute anything that is wrong. A single wrong default or an account that is not yours can pull a score down sharply.

Step 2: work on what lenders see

Pay on time, every month.

Payment history is the biggest factor. Set debit orders for the day after payday.

Keep card and facility balances low.

Using less of your limit looks better than maxing it out.

Apply less often.

Each application can add an enquiry. Several in a short time can look like you are struggling.

Keep older accounts open

if they are in good standing and cost you nothing.

Update your details.

Wrong addresses or employers make it harder for lenders to match you.

Why your scores differ

Each bureau uses its own model and may hold slightly different information. A different number at each bureau is normal. Look at the trend, not one number.

Judgment removal

Understand the difference between the court order and the bureau record, and which one you need to deal with.

A judgment is a court order that you owe money. The credit bureau listing is a record of that order. They are linked but not the same thing, and the route to remove each is different.

Your options

The judgment is wrong.

For example you were never served, or it is for someone else. Dispute the bureau entry and get legal advice about rescinding the judgment.

You have paid it.

Paid-up judgments must be removed from bureau records. Get a paid-up letter from the creditor or their attorney and send it to the bureaus.

You want it set aside.

A court can rescind a judgment, often with the creditor’s written consent once the debt is paid. This needs an attorney or a court application.

It is old.

A judgment can only show for up to 5 years on your report.

Check it first

Many people think they have a judgment when the report actually shows an adverse classification or a debt review flag. Get your report and look at the "judgments" section before you pay anyone.

My application reference is [reference].

Kind regards

[Your name]

[Your phone number]

2. Get your free credit report

Payment profile updates

Tell a wrong missed-payment entry apart from accurate late-payment history, and fix the wrong ones.

Your payment profile is the month-by-month record of how each account was paid. It is factual information and can show for up to 5 years. That means a real missed payment stays. A missed payment that never happened should not.

Common errors

A payment you made on time is shown as missed or late.

An arrangement you agreed with the lender is shown as arrears.

A debit order failed because of the bank, not you, and was never corrected.

An account you closed still shows months of non-payment.

What to gather

Bank statements or proof of payment for the months in question, any written arrangement with the lender, and your latest credit report. Keep these yourself. Send only what the bureau or lender asks for, through their official channel.

How to dispute

Get your free report from the bureau that shows the entry. You get one free report from each of TransUnion

, Experian

, XDS

and VeriCred

every 12 months.

Write down the credit provider, the last 4 digits of the account number and exactly what is wrong.

Lodge a free dispute with that bureau, online or by email. Attach your proof. Ask for a dispute reference number.

The bureau has 20 business days to check with the credit provider and either show evidence or correct the entry.

If nothing changes, or you disagree with the outcome, complain to the National Financial Ombud Scheme

. It is free.

How we help

Prescribed accounts

Check the dates and the evidence before you pay, acknowledge or dispute an old debt.

Most consumer debts prescribe after 3 years. Prescription means the debt is extinguished: nobody may continue to collect it, sell it or "reactivate" it. But the 3 years can be interrupted, so the dates matter.

What interrupts prescription

You acknowledged the debt, for example by paying something or signing an acknowledgement of debt.

The creditor served a summons on you.

A court judgment was granted (a judgment debt prescribes after 30 years).

Before you reply to a collector

Do not pay or sign anything on a debt you think has prescribed until you have checked the dates. A small "goodwill" payment can be argued as an acknowledgement.

Prescription and your credit report

Prescription and your credit report are separate questions. A prescribed debt can still show as history if it is within its display period, but it should not be shown as an amount you still owe. If it is, dispute it with the bureau.

What we do

Check the last payment date, any acknowledgement and any summons.

Work out whether prescription is likely.

Give you a dispute or response message that fits.
